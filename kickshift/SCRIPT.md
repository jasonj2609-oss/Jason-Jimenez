# KickShift — Speaker Script (~8 min)

Tip: the big title on each slide is your cue. Read it, then explain it in your own words. Press **N** in the HTML deck to see these notes while rehearsing.

## 1. Intro: lights on  `0:00 – 0:30`

*(Room quiet. Let the black screen and the light play.)*

"Introducing..."

*(Wait for the headlights to turn on.)*

"KickShift. A car built to score."

## 2. The idea  `0:30 – 0:50`

"Everyone loves soccer, and everyone loves supercars. But car soccer only exists in video games."

"So we asked: what if the car was the player?"

## 3. What we are pitching  `0:50 – 1:10`

"KickShift is a new sport with three parts: the car, the arena, and the game."

"Your investment builds a small test version: two cars and one test arena."

## 4. The car: not just a car  `1:10 – 2:05`

"It looks like a supercar, but inside it's built for the game."

*(Point at each label as it appears.)*

"A coil underneath picks up power from the floor. A supercapacitor stores it for a quick boost. A battery runs the car all match. Motors in every wheel give the push."

"The nose swaps out when it gets hit. Markers let cameras track the car. And a light bar shows everyone how much energy you have."

## 5. The arena: the field is the charger  `2:05 – 2:50`

"This is the arena, and this is what we sell."

"The floor has charging lanes. They light up and charge a car only when it drives over them."

"Cameras on the roof track every car and the ball, so the game referees itself. The scoreboard shows everyone's energy, and glass walls keep the fans safe."

## 6. Physics 1: wireless charging  `2:50 – 3:25`

"How does the floor charge the car? It's the same idea as a wireless phone charger."

"Coils in the floor make a magnetic field that keeps flipping. A changing magnetic field through the car's coil makes electricity. That's Faraday's law, equation 1."

"The graph shows it: electricity only appears while the car is over the lane."

## 7. Physics 2: battery and supercapacitor  `3:25 – 4:00`

"The car stores energy two ways."

"The battery is like a big water tank: it holds a lot, but fills and empties slowly. The supercapacitor is like a bucket: it holds less, but fills and empties almost instantly."

"So the battery lasts the whole match, and the supercapacitor is for quick boosts. Ours holds about 1,000 joules."

## 8. Physics 3: the boost  `4:00 – 4:35`

"When you boost, the wheels push back on the floor, and the floor pushes the car forward. That's Newton's third law."

"But there's a limit: grip. Push too hard and the tyres just spin, like a car on ice. So more power doesn't always mean more speed."

## 9. Physics 4: one pass  `4:35 – 5:05`

"Is one trip over a lane enough for a boost?"

"One pass gives about 1,000 joules. A boost needs about 500. So even if half the energy is lost along the way, one pass pays for one boost."

## 10. Physics 5: energy is a tactic  `5:05 – 5:40`

"Here's the twist: the slower you drive over a lane, the more energy you collect, because you're on it longer."

"But if you go slow, the other team might get the ball first. That choice is the game."

"Collect power from the field, store it, and decide when to spend it."

## 11. Business model  `5:40 – 6:20`

"How does it make money? We sell arenas to places like go-kart tracks."

"Players pay about $20 to play. The venue pays us $400,000 for the arena, plus a monthly fee and a small cut of every game."

*(Point to the chart.) "One venue makes about $359,000 a year, so it earns back the arena in about a year."*

## 12. Costs  `6:20 – 6:40`

"It's cheap to run: a whole game uses less than a dollar of electricity."

"An arena costs us $250,000 to build, and we sell it for $400,000."

## 13. Roadmap  `6:40 – 7:00`

"First we build two test cars, test the charging lanes, and play 50 test games. That's the first year."

"Then we open our own arena, and then we sell arenas to other places."

## 14. Projections  `7:00 – 7:20`

"These are predictions, not promises."

"By year 5 we could have 35 arenas and about $10.8 million a year in sales, and we start making a profit in year 4."

## 15. The numbers  `7:20 – 7:45`

"Here's everything on one page: what it costs on the left, and how it makes money on the right."

"The big takeaway: a venue earns its money back in about a year, and KickShift makes a profit by year 4."

## 16. Close  `7:45 – 8:00`

*(Let the car light up first.)*

"Collect power from the field, store it, and decide when to spend it. That's KickShift: a car built to score."

"Thank you. Any questions?"

### Physics cheat-sheet (for Q&A)

| Idea | Equation | Numbers used |
|---|---|---|
| Faraday's law | ε = −N dΦ/dt | AC in the lane coils changes the flux through the car's receiver coil |
| Energy collected | E = P t | 1 kW for 1 s (4 m lane at 4 m/s) = 1,000 J |
| Capacitor energy | E = ½ C V² | 0.87 F at 48 V ≈ 1,000 J |
| Grip limit | F = τ/r ≤ μ m g | m = 50 kg, μ = 0.9 → 441 N; r = 0.15 m → τ ≈ 66 N·m |
| Boost energy | ΔE = ½ m (v_f² − v_i²) | ½(50)(6² − 4²) = 500 J |
| Tactics | E = P L / v | 2 m/s → 2,000 J; 4 m/s → 1,000 J; 8 m/s → 500 J |
| Match energy | E = ∫ P dt | ≈ 1.5 kWh per car → ≈ $0.25 at $0.16/kWh |

### Likely question: "What about damage to the cars? Does it still make money?"

Say: "The first cars are 50 kilograms and remote-driven. A full-speed hit carries about 900 joules, around 200 times less than a real car. The front is a bumper shell that's made to be crushed and swapped for $150, like a hockey stick. Even if damage costs a venue $70,000 a year, it still makes about $290,000 and earns back its arena in under a year and a half. And every replacement shell is something we sell."

- Physics: KE = ½mv² = ½ × 50 × 6² = 900 J (a 900 kg car at 20 m/s has 180,000 J). Impulse: F = Δp/Δt; p = 300 kg·m/s, so stopping in 5 ms means about 60 kN, while a shell that crushes over 30 ms brings it down to about 10 kN.
- Cost per venue per year (estimate): 33,600 car-matches; one shell per 100 matches = 336 shells × $150 = $50K; other repairs about $18K (25% of the $72K fleet). Total about $68K, so the venue keeps about $290K and pays back in about 17 months (about 22 months if damage is double).
- Upside: shells are a consumable we sell (about $50K a year per venue; at roughly $40 each to make, about $37K profit). This is not in the projections.
- Full-size cars come later, with racing crash structures, and sponsors and the league fund repairs. The first year's 50 test matches measure the real damage rate.

Biggest risks (be ready to name them): wireless-lane efficiency on moving cars, bumper durability, safety around crowds, and whether venues buy. The seed round tests the first three before big money is spent.

Sources and caveats: the prototype numbers are illustrative. Supercapacitor and battery ranges are typical published values. Purdue University has reported wireless power transfer to a moving truck at highway speed; that supports the principle, not KickShift's specific design.
